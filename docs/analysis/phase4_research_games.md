# Phase 4 research: what to borrow from RTS/RPG hybrids, loot games and hobby-RTS tech

Research notes for the Magic Carpet 1 port's new mode (PvP RTS/RPG hybrid, wizard on carpet, castles + towns, summoned monster armies, RPG loot layer, offline bots + online). Written 2026-10-07. Sources are cited inline; where a primary source was unreachable (Fandom wikis block fetching) the fact is marked *(unverified, from memory)* and should be checked before being relied on for numbers.

Conventions used below: "MC" = Magic Carpet; "the mode" = the Phase 4 game mode; "wizard" = the player's carpet-riding avatar; "castle" = the base-game castle that grows with mana; "lair" = the new unit-summoning building.

---

## 1. Sacrifice (Shiny, 2000)

The closest existing game to what we are building: a wizard avatar who is simultaneously the commander, with no worker economy. Metacritic 89 but commercially a flop; its failures are as instructive as its successes.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice_(video_game) , https://gamesfirst.com/articles/2001/preview-sacrifice/ , https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/shinys-game-sacrifice-2000/53393 , https://sacrifice-shiny.fandom.com/wiki/Convert

### Core loop
- You are a wizard seen from behind (third person). Mana regenerates constantly; souls are the finite resource. Every creature costs 1..5 souls depending on its tier *(cost = tier, Titans = 5; unverified from memory, consistent with the Convert formula below)*. When your creature dies its souls float free as blue souls and drift back to you / your altar; enemy creature deaths leave red souls that you can only take by casting **Convert**: a Sac Doctor "operates" on the corpse for `souls x 30 s` (30 s for a 1-soul creature, 150 s for a Titan) and then carries the caged soul back to your altar. Kill the Sac Doctor during the operation and the red souls reappear; kill him on the way home and the creature breaks free, partially healed, back under the enemy's control (https://sacrifice-shiny.fandom.com/wiki/Convert).
- Mana regen is faster near your altar and near **mana fountains**. You claim a fountain by erecting a **Manalith** on it; Manahoar creatures channel manalith mana to the wizard. Manaliths are the territory layer: multiplayer mode "Domination" is literally "hold N manaliths".
- Win condition: walk a friendly creature onto the enemy **altar** and sacrifice it; Sac Doctors perform the ritual, killing them interrupts it. Desecration banishes the wizard permanently. Wizards who are merely killed in the field are not out: the wizard re-forms at his altar after a delay and keeps his souls/creatures *(unverified from memory)*. This is why the altar, not the wizard's HP, is the real objective.
- Three creature classes in a triangle: melee beats ranged, ranged beats flyers, flyers beat melee. Plus utility creatures (Manahoar, Sac Doctor) and ~9 creature levels per god.
- Commands: context menus, hotkeys and **mouse gestures** for move/attack/guard/follow; formations (line, circle, guard, phalanx); creature groups. Campaign let you pick a god per mission, accumulating a cross-god spellbook ("tune the spells you bring into the game" is what the quartertothree thread remembers fondly).
- Multiplayer modes: Skirmish (banish everyone), Domination (manaliths), Slaughter (kills), Soul Harvest (souls). 4 players max.

### Mechanics worth borrowing, mapped to MC
1. **Soul economy as unit cap + currency + comeback mechanic.** In MC terms: every monster summoned from the lair consumes *essence* (souls) and the essence returns to you when the monster dies *unless* an enemy wizard reaches the corpse and casts a conversion ritual first. This creates the "fight over the corpses" mini-game that made Sacrifice battles tense, and naturally bounds army size without a pop-cap UI. Suggested numbers: start with 8 essence, tier 1 = 1, tier 2 = 2, tier 3 = 3, elite/boss summons = 5; conversion ritual 20 s x tier, interruptible by killing the ritual caster. Essence pickups in the wilderness (like Sacrifice's unclaimed souls) give early-game exploration a purpose.
2. **Manalith = fountain capture.** MC already has mana as the win resource and a castle that stores it; add a scattering of *mana springs* in the procedural world that can be claimed by building a small shrine on them. Shrines raise mana regen for any friendly wizard nearby and feed the owning castle slowly. They are the natural "territory" objective and bot waypoint, and "hold 5 shrines for 3 minutes" is a ready-made alternate win condition.
3. **Altar desecration rather than "kill the player".** Map it to the MC castle: the castle cannot be destroyed outright (it can in the base game, by balloons/attacks), but an enemy wizard who lands a unit or himself in the courtyard channels a 15-30 s *desecration*; interrupt by killing/possessing the channeler. The defender's death only costs a respawn timer at the castle. This keeps PvP from being decided by a single lucky duel and gives everyone a "home base" reason to come back.
4. **Wizard respawn at the altar with souls intact.** Death should cost time + position, not economy, otherwise the snowball Sacrifice's reviewers complained about ("players losing early battles couldn't recover") gets worse.
5. **Mouse gestures / radial commands from the avatar.** Sacrifice proved you can issue RTS orders without leaving a third-person view. We additionally have the RTS overview camera, so first-person needs only four orders (attack-move here, guard me, hold, return to lair), bound to the controller D-pad or mouse gestures.
6. **Rock-paper-scissors creature classes.** MC monsters already divide into ground melee (worms, crabs, trolls), ranged (archers, skeletons, wyverns' fireballs) and flyers (bees, vultures, dragons, genies). Formalising the triangle with explicit 1.5x damage bonuses gives the RTS layer counter-play instantly.
7. **Pick-your-spellbook before the match.** Sacrifice's "god per mission" became a loadout. For us: a wizard chooses 6 of N spells to bring, with the rest only findable as drops in-match (ties into the RPG layer).

### Pitfalls / what players disliked
- Third-person view hid the battlefield; "frenetic combat" needed constant attention; Maximum PC's "best argument for a third hand". Our RTS camera is precisely the fix, but it must be a *one-key toggle* and orders must be issuable from both views.
- No comeback: "Players losing early battles couldn't recover to win" and multiplayer became "long-drawn stalemates" (Wikipedia reception). Souls that never come back to the loser compound this; our essence should return to the summoner on a timer even if the enemy does not convert it, and the convert mechanic should be the only way to permanently remove essence.
- Reviewers said tactics boiled down to "continually summoning groups of creatures". A town/resource layer plus hero progression gives more axes than Sacrifice had.
- "Unit AI questionable, pathing iffy, poor camera" (quartertothree). Unit autonomy (auto-retaliate, auto-return-to-lair when idle and hurt) matters more in an avatar game because the player is often looking elsewhere.
- Small team, niche hybrid, bad marketing: the design needs to be sold as "Magic Carpet with armies", not as an RTS.

## 2. Warcraft III (Blizzard, 2002/2003)

Sources: https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/upkeep.shtml , https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/heroes.shtml , https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/daynight.shtml , https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/specialcommands.shtml , https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Creeps , https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lost_Temple_(Warcraft_III) , https://www.hiveworkshop.com/threads/how-does-ai-difficulty-influence-ai-micro-and-unit-controls.294489 , https://github.com/SMUnlimited/AMAI/wiki

### Core loop
Gather gold (5 workers per mine) and lumber, build a tech tree with three tiers of town hall, train 1-3 heroes, farm neutral creep camps for XP and items while building a small army (upkeep caps the practical size at ~50-80 food), fight over expansions, kill the enemy buildings. 1v1 games typically run 15-30 minutes.

### Mechanics worth borrowing
1. **Upkeep as an anti-turtle tax.** 0-50 food: 100% income; 51-80: 70%; 81-100: 40%. Blizzard's stated rationale: "players are more aggressive and turtle far less", and "the more units allowed, the less powerful Heroes will be relative to your army"; High Upkeep "is MEANT to be very punishing" (https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/upkeep.shtml). MC mapping: every monster alive drains a trickle of castle mana per second that rises in two steps (e.g. 0 below 20 essence in play, -30% castle income at 21-35, -60% above 35). This keeps the wizard the star, keeps unit counts inside the sprite/pool budget of the engine, and discourages sitting at home with a mega-army.
2. **Hero XP curve and creep XP decay.** XP to reach level L = previous + 100 x L (200, 500, 900, 1400, 2000, 2700, 3500, 4400, 5400 at level 10). Creep XP is cut to 80% at level 1 and declines to 0% by level 5 (TFT) so farming neutrals cannot carry a hero to max level; past level 5 only player-controlled kills give XP (https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/heroes.shtml). Hero kills give 100 (lvl 1) to 800 XP (lvl 10). One ability point per level, ultimate at level 6, level cap 10. This is a good template for the wizard: ~10 levels in a 30-40 minute match, early levels from neutral monsters, later levels only from PvP.
3. **Hero revive cost scales with level** (about half hero cost + 10%/level, capped 550 gold; revive time capped at 110 s; Tavern instant revive at 50% HP for a premium). Wizard death penalty in MC = respawn time at castle scaling with level (say 10 s + 5 s/level) plus a flat mana fee; an "emergency revive" that costs stored mana keeps late-game deaths from being game-ending.
4. **Creep camps with item drops tiered by camp difficulty.** Items have levels 1-8; 7+ are "artifacts". Map-makers match drop level to camp difficulty and to the value of what the camp guards: nearest expansion drops a level-1 permanent, shop creeps a level-3 charged, fountain creeps a level-4 charged (Lost Temple notes, https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lost_Temple_(Warcraft_III)). Charged items (consumables with N charges) make up most drops; permanents are rarer. MC mapping: wild monster lairs in the procedural world, graded 1-5 by distance from spawns, dropping mana, scrolls (charged spell casts) and rarely permanent spell upgrades; elite named monsters guard the mana springs.
5. **Neutral buildings.** Mercenary Camp (buy neutral units), Goblin Lab (zeppelins, sappers, map reveal), Tavern (neutral heroes), Fountain of Health (1% HP/s regen nearby) (https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fountain_of_Health). MC mapping: ruined temples that sell a one-off summon or spell for mana; healing springs; a "wild lair" you can capture to summon that monster type.
6. **Day/night.** 480 s cycle (240 day / 240 night); night cuts sight range sharply and most creeps sleep until attacked or a building goes up nearby (https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/daynight.shtml). MC already has a sky; a day/night cycle with sleeping wild monsters makes night the raid window and daytime the creeping window.
7. **Control UX.** Control groups 0-9, Tab cycles sub-groups within a mixed selection, Shift-queue, rally points per production building, "select all of type" by Ctrl-click (https://classic.battle.net/war3/basics/specialcommands.shtml). We need at least: 4 groups, select-all-army, select-idle, lair rally point, Tab to cycle types.
8. **AI structure.** The stock melee AI is a JASS script: a build order list (`SetBuildUnit`), attack-wave lists sized per difficulty, hero skill order, with difficulty constants EASY/NORMAL/HARD; Insane is just HARD plus a resource cheat ("outspend you twofold"), and the AI "always knows where the expansions are" (https://www.hiveworkshop.com/threads/how-does-ai-difficulty-influence-ai-micro-and-unit-controls.294489). The AI does not really micro, rush or creep efficiently and is weak to air harass. AMAI (community mod) adds personality profiles, dynamic strategy switching, harassment routines and team coordination (https://github.com/SMUnlimited/AMAI/wiki). Lesson: a scripted build order + wave-based attacker + cheat-scaled difficulty is enough for a hobby bot; invest the saved effort in *personality* and *harassment* because that is what players notice.

### Pitfalls
- WC3's economy demands a lot of macro APM; this is why the RTS layer in our mode should mostly run itself (towns grow organically, villagers auto-assign) and the player's attention goes to the wizard and the army.
- Hero snowballing: the first hero to level 3-4 wins many WC3 games. The creep-XP decay and the hero-kill XP table counter that; copy both.
- Blizzard removed Insane's cheating twice in forum experiments and players found the AI "a walk in the park" without it (https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/warcraft3/t/upkeep-mechanic-removal-test-it/1043). Accept resource cheats for the hardest bot but hide them behind flavour ("ancient wizard has a larger castle").

## 3. Dungeon Keeper 1/2 (Bullfrog, 1997/1999)

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Keeper_2 , https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Happiness (summary via search), https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/d-first-person-mode , https://keeperfx.net/wiki/Level-Script-Commands , https://keeper.lubiki.pl/dk2_docs/dk2_editor_manual.htm

### Core loop
Dig out rooms; rooms attract creature types through a Portal (first portal = 15 creatures, each extra portal +5, per the DK2 editor manual); imps (free, tireless workers) dig, claim and haul gold; pay creatures from the treasury on payday; train in the Training Room (DK2: training only to level 4, levels 5-10 need real combat or the Combat Pit); keep creatures happy; repel hero invasions and kill rival Keepers' Dungeon Hearts. Possession lets you drive any creature in first person.

### Mechanics worth borrowing
1. **Possession.** DK1: free, always available; DK2: a 500-mana spell. The payoff is explicit: "a possessed minion can fight much more efficiently since the AI doesn't dodge attacks or use abilities optimally", dragons' fire breath becomes continuous, and possessed creatures can walk through enemy territory/lava for early heart strikes; rogues cloak, trolls spot traps, dark elves snipe (https://www.ludo.guide/guide/dungeon-keeper-2/d-first-person-mode). MC *already has a Possession spell* (round 7-9 notes); extend it so that possessing a summoned monster in the mode gives that monster a special skill (dragon: sustained flame, bee: swarm dash, worm: burrow) and lets the wizard "ride" into fights his carpet would not survive. Cost it in mana and make the wizard's body a vulnerable target while possessing.
2. **Rooms attract creature types (building as recruitment filter).** Instead of a tech tree you build the *habitat* (hatchery + lair + training room draws warlocks, graveyard draws vampires). MC mapping: the lair's summon list is gated by what the town has: a hive building unlocks bees, a quarry unlocks trolls/crabs, a swamp pool unlocks worms, a spire unlocks dragons. Towns growing organically is exactly how MC already works, so "grow the town to attract monsters" is a natural fit and avoids an alien tech-tree UI.
3. **Imps as free autonomous workers** who need no pay/food and pick their own jobs by proximity. MC villagers should behave the same: no manual assignment, just a per-building slider or a "priority" toggle, with the DK-style Hand pick-up-and-drop to force a villager/monster somewhere. The DK "hand" maps directly onto the RTS overview camera: click-grab a unit, drop it.
4. **Creature happiness/needs with consequences.** Anger from unpaid wages, no lair, hunger, idleness (Horned Reaper), or standing on an ally's corpse; angry creatures vandalise, fight allies or leave; remedies are gold on the head, the Temple, or a bigger lair; torture lowers it except for Mistresses (https://dungeonkeeper.fandom.com/wiki/Happiness). For the mode: give monsters a *loyalty* bar fed by castle mana upkeep (see WC3 upkeep) and food from the town; unpaid monsters go neutral and start eating villagers. That links the town economy to the army without a pop cap and produces readable drama (health bars + a loyalty icon).
5. **Portal-limited arrival rate.** The portal count throttles army growth and makes extra portals a territorial objective (first portal 15 creatures, +5 per extra). Our lair should summon slowly (one monster per N seconds) and extra lairs/claimed wild lairs raise the rate.
6. **Training levels 1-10 with the training room capped at 4** (DK2) so veterans must earn levels in combat: monsters that survive fights become rare named elites, which players then protect and the enemy wants to kill (this is where the RPG health bars/names earn their keep).
7. **Computer Keeper architecture (via KeeperFX).** The DK1 AI is a table-driven set of *processes* ("DIG TO GOLD", "BUILD A TREASURE ROOM", "BUILD A TRAINING ROOM" ... with priority, room size, prerequisites), *checks* run on a repeat interval ("check for imps", "check money" ...) and *events* ("dungeon breach" style sudden situations that spawn a process), with globals like dig-stack size, processes-time, click-rate, max-room-build-tasks and sims-before-dig; 16+ preset player models mix aggressive/defensive/room-size/no-digging flavours (https://keeperfx.net/wiki/Level-Script-Commands). This is an excellent, proven low-cost bot architecture for a base-building game: a priority list of build "processes", periodic "checks" that enqueue them, and "events" that pre-empt. Copy it.

### Pitfalls
- DK's micro-management of unhappy creatures could be tedious; keep needs to one bar and two remedies.
- Possession in DK was a toy for many players because the overview was more efficient; ours is different because first-person *is* the base game. Make the overview the limited view (fog, cooldown-free but no spellcasting except a few "global" spells) so the avatar view stays primary.
- Fortified walls in DK1 were impenetrable (removed in DK2): avoid un-crackable defences; everything must be breakable by a determined, mana-heavy attack.

## 4. Diablo II (2000) and briefly Path of Exile

Sources: https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/monsters/bonus.shtml , https://www.purediablo.com/d2wiki/Treasure_class , https://diablo-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Magic_Find_(Diablo_II) , https://almarsguides.com/Computer/Games/Diablo2/Farming/MagicFind/ , https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Monster_modifiers , https://game-wisdom.com/critical/loot-2-0-and-diablos-fourth-revival , https://www.psu.com/news/the-slot-machine-psyche-how-variable-ratio-reinforcement-drives-modern-gaming-engagement/ , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861872

### Core loop
Kill monster packs, watch items spray out, identify, compare, repeat; difficulty tiers (Normal/Nightmare/Hell) re-run the same content with harder monsters and better drop tables; monster packs are spiced by champion and unique variants that are visibly different and drop more.

### Mechanics worth borrowing
1. **Monster rarity tiers with affixes.**
   - *Champions* spawn in packs of 2-4; all but one are plain Champions (+90/75/66% damage by difficulty, +20 speed), the last is one of Berserker (+270% damage, 25% of a champion's life, x5 XP), Fanatic (+100 speed, -70% defence), Ghostly (cold damage, 80% physical resist) or Possessed (double life, cannot be cursed); champions give x3 XP (https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/monsters/bonus.shtml).
   - *Unique (boss) monsters* get 1/2/3 random modifiers in Normal/Nightmare/Hell from an equal-chance list: Extra Strong, Extra Fast, Cursed, Magic Resistant, Fire/Cold/Lightning Enchanted, Mana Burn, Multishot, Spectral Hit, Stone Skin, Teleport, Aura; resist mods are skipped if they would create a third immunity. Their *minions* get x2/1.75/1.5 life, +3 levels and x5 XP and inherit some mods.
   - *Drops*: uniques and super-uniques "always drop either an item of at least magical quality or a gem or rune" (https://almarsguides.com/Computer/Games/Diablo2/Farming/MagicFind/).
   - MC mapping: wild monsters spawn as Normal / Champion pack / **Named Elite** (random name like "Gorrak the Skyburner", 2-3 affixes drawn from a list of ~12 that reuse MC's own effects: *Fire-breathing* (wyvern-style fireballs), *Swift*, *Armoured* (stone skin), *Mana-burning* (drains wizard mana on hit), *Lightning-enchanted* (chain bolt on death), *Volcanic* (spawns a small volcano on death), *Teleporting*, *Splitting* (dies into 2 lesser), *Aura* (buffs nearby monsters)). Elite kills guarantee a drop of at least "magic" tier.
2. **Treasure classes.** A hierarchical table: each monster rolls a TC; the TC picks a sub-TC or item; then quality is rolled (normal -> magic -> rare -> set -> unique) with Magic Find only affecting the quality roll, never the item type, and with diminishing returns on unique (https://www.purediablo.com/d2wiki/Treasure_class). *NoDrop* chance falls with more players nearby. For MC: a small TC tree (`Elite_T3 -> {mana_orb 40%, scroll 35%, upgrade 20%, spell 5%}`) with a per-tier quality roll is simple to implement and tune; "NoDrop shrinks with players nearby" encourages group fights at elites.
3. **Rarity colour language**: white/blue/yellow/green/gold is universally understood. Use it for health bar borders of Normal/Champion/Elite/Boss monsters and for drop beams.
4. **Variable-ratio reinforcement + near-misses.** The compulsive part is unpredictable reward timing, not reward size; "the brain responds to the pattern of delivery rather than the value of the payout" (https://www.psu.com/news/the-slot-machine-psyche-how-variable-ratio-reinforcement-drives-modern-gaming-engagement/). Near-misses increase persistence (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2861872). Practical rules: drops must be *visible and audible* at the moment of the kill (beam, sound, floating "+120 mana"), elites must *telegraph* their rarity before the fight (name plate, aura) so the anticipation builds, and the rare-tier chance should be low but with a hidden *pity counter* (Diablo 3 later added bad-luck protection) so a session always ends with at least one good moment.
5. **Diablo 3 Loot 2.0 lessons**: fewer but better drops, "smart loot" biased to the finder's class, legendaries made unambiguously best (https://game-wisdom.com/critical/loot-2-0-and-diablos-fourth-revival). For us: drop fewer items but make each one a *decision* (spell upgrade A or B? which spell slot?); bias spell-upgrade drops toward spells the wizard actually has equipped.
6. **Path of Exile**: magic monsters have 1 affix, rares 3 affixes (one may be an aura) and a random generated name; rarity multiplies life, damage, XP, drop rarity and quantity (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Monster_modifiers). The reusable idea is that *every* affix is a mechanic the player can see and react to, never a hidden stat multiplier only.
7. **Difficulty scaling by re-running the map at Nightmare/Hell.** For a replayable mode: a post-match "ascension" tier for offline play where wild monsters gain affixes and bots get faster builds, unlocked by winning the previous tier.

### Pitfalls
- Dead items: if most drops are vendor trash players stop looking. Keep the drop list tiny (mana, scrolls, upgrades, spells) and make even "mana" useful (it is the win resource).
- Hidden math breeds distrust; show the elite's affixes on its name plate.
- D2's monster immunities in Hell are widely hated; never make an elite immune to a whole spell school, only resistant.

## 5. Other hybrids, one paragraph each

**SpellForce: The Order of Dawn (2003).** Avatar RPG hero (levels, skills, item gating) plus full RTS base building; up to 5 rune heroes travel with you (https://www.rpgfan.com/review/spellforce-the-order-of-dawn/ , https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/spellforce-the-order-of-dawn-review/). Lesson: letting the player choose between "solve it with the hero" and "solve it with the army" per situation is the fun part; the un-fun part is that both halves were full-fat and the RTS half dragged (workers, 6 resources). Keep the RTS half thin.

**Savage 1/2 (2003/2008) and Natural Selection 2.** One player is a top-down commander, the rest are FPS/melee fighters; "the first definite RTS/FPS hybrid" with an "extreme learning curve" and launch without tutorials (https://gamesfirst.com/articles/2003/savage-the-battle-for-newerth/ , https://www.gamewatcher.com/savage-the-battle-for-newerth/review). The recurring problem: the commander role is unpopular, hard to learn, and a bad commander ruins a team; Savage 2 deliberately limited commander power (https://www.shacknews.com/article/49862/savage-2-a-tortured-soul). Lesson for us: do *not* split commander and avatar across players; every player is both, and the overview camera must be optional rather than a job.

**Battlezone (1998).** First-person tank + RTS where the entire interface runs from the cockpit: put the crosshair on a unit, press Space for a context menu with a default order pre-selected, point at the target, press again (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlezone_(1998_video_game) , https://www.gamespot.com/battlezone-1998/reviews/battlezone-review-2538156/). Economy is scrap collected by scavenger vehicles from the battlefield. Reviewers called the interface "innovative and simple". Lesson: two-press ordering (select by look, confirm on target) is the template for our first-person command set; scrap-from-wreckage = corpses leaving mana/essence for whoever collects them.

**Northgard (2018).** Map divided into tiles; you may only build in a tile you have colonised by spending food, cost rising per tile; five resources all gathered by assigning villagers to buildings; three months of winter each year cut food, raise wood use and weaken warriors (https://pcgamer.com/northgard-review , https://www.thegamer.com/northgard-beginners-tips/). Lessons: territory as discrete claimable tiles is readable from an overview and cheap to simulate; a periodic global hazard (winter) forces everyone to plan and creates natural match phases. MC analogue: a recurring *mana eclipse* every ~6 minutes during which castle mana regen stops and wild monsters get bolder, plus tile-based town claims around the castle.

**Majesty (2000).** No direct unit control; heroes have personalities and act autonomously; the player places bounty flags (attack/explore, later protect/fear) and heroes decide whether the gold is worth the risk, high-level heroes demanding more (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majesty_2:_The_Fantasy_Kingdom_Sim , https://gamebanshee.com/a84). Lesson: *indirect* orders are great for a player whose attention is elsewhere. Our monsters should default to Majesty-style autonomy (guard their lair, hunt nearby wild monsters, retreat when hurt) and the RTS layer can add a "bounty flag" order: drop a mana bounty on a point and idle monsters go there on their own.

**Black & White (2001).** Influence ring around each temple/village grows with population and belief; you may only cast miracles and build inside it; villagers raise desire flags (food, wood, then offspring and expansion once satisfied) at the Village Store; worshippers at the temple generate prayer power (https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Influence , https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Village_Store , https://www.pcgamer.com/reinstall-black-and-white/). Lesson: an *influence radius* tied to town size is the cleanest way to bound where a player may build and where cheap spells work; desire flags are a self-explaining UI for an organically growing town. MC: castle + town centre project an influence circle; spells inside are cheaper; building outside it is impossible.

**Populous: The Beginning (1998).** The shaman is a weak avatar who respawns at a fixed Reincarnation Site after a timer "provided there are enough followers remaining"; huts grow and breed more followers the fuller they are; mana comes from population; braves build and are trained into warriors, firewarriors, preachers and spies; spells are learned by worshipping at stone heads (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous:_The_Beginning , https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/buildings-structures/index.html). Lessons: respawn gated by surviving population keeps elimination possible without "kill the hero" being the win; spells discovered at world landmarks = our rare spell drops; converting neutral wild men = capturing wild lairs.

**Tooth and Tail (2017).** An "arcade RTS" built pad-first: your commander is the cursor; tap to rally all troops to you, hold to focus a target, triggers switch between one unit type and the whole army; three buttons and a stick; building happens on fixed plots around farms (https://www.pcgamer.com/tooth-and-tail-review/ , https://kotaku.com/tooth-and-tail-makes-strategy-games-approachable-1804254719). This is the proof that avatar-as-cursor plus "come to me / go there" plus a type filter is enough RTS for a pad. Our first-person control set should be exactly this: *rally to me*, *attack what I'm looking at*, *cycle unit type*, *return home*.

**Age of Empires II villager economy.** Villagers gather at fixed rates (~0.4 food/s per farmer), must walk resources back to a drop-off building so drop-off distance is the main efficiency lever, and "idle villager" is the key UI button; pros keep villagers at 50-65% of pop (https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Villager_(Age_of_Empires) , https://west-games.com/aoe2-resource-calculator/). Lessons: have the town auto-place drop-off storehouses and auto-reassign idle villagers; expose one global slider ("villagers: mana / food / lumber / ore") rather than per-villager orders. MC already simulates village growth; we add a resource walk with visible carrying villagers so players can *see* the economy in first person.

**Battle royale pacing (Apex, Fortnite).** Apex's ring: six rounds with wait/close times of 1:15/4:20, 2:00/1:05, 1:30/0:45, 1:30/0:40, 1:15/0:50, 1:00/2:00 and damage per tick 3, 4, 10, 15, 20, 25; the ring exists because "practice matches were often drawn out overnight because some competitors preferred to stay put" (https://apexlegends.wiki.gg/wiki/Ring). Three defining mechanics: large lobby, shrinking zone, everyone starts with nothing. Lessons even for a non-BR mode: a *scheduled* global pressure that escalates in damage and shrinks the safe area is the simplest anti-stall device ever shipped; loot that resets/relocates with the zone pulls players together. If the mode keeps a BR variant: the "storm" is a spreading mana blight that kills villagers and crops outside the circle, forcing towns to be abandoned and armies to march; a 25-30 minute schedule in 5-6 steps.

## 6. Practical topics

### 6.1 Unit control that works on keyboard+mouse and gamepad
Sources: https://waywardstrategy.com/2020/03/23/halo-wars-the-ultimate-design-for-console-rts/ , https://www.halopedia.org/Circle_menu , https://gameinformer.com/games/stormrise/b/xbox360/archive/2009/09/22/review.aspx , Tooth and Tail reviews above.

- Halo Wars (Ensemble, 2009) is the reference console scheme: A tap = select unit, A double-tap = select all of that type, A hold = paint-brush selection, dedicated buttons for *select all on screen* and *select all everywhere*, a sub-select strip to filter types, a stick-driven radial (circle) menu for building, and bases with 7 fixed building slots instead of free placement. Halo Wars 2 added custom groups and hold position. Stormrise's "rotate a cone of light to pick units" is the negative example: "destroys almost all of the game's playability".
- Recommended scheme for the mode:
  - **First person (both inputs):** D-pad/number keys 1-4 = *Rally to me*, *Attack-move to reticle*, *Hold/guard here*, *Return to lair*; LB/Tab cycles which unit type the order applies to (all / bees / dragons ...); Battlezone-style two-press for a specific target. No free cursor at all in first person.
  - **Overview (RTS camera):** mouse drag-box or pad paint-select; A/left-click on a unit twice = all of type; four control groups on pad = hold RB + face button; radial menu for building placed on *fixed plots* around the town centre (Halo Wars / Tooth and Tail) so no fiddly placement; one global rally flag per lair.
  - **Autonomy fills the gap:** units default to guard/retaliate, hurt units return home, idle villagers self-assign. The better the autonomy, the fewer buttons needed.
  - **Latency masking:** acknowledge every order instantly with a sound and a ground marker even though lockstep delays execution (see 6.2).

### 6.2 Netcode: deterministic lockstep for an RTS
Sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond , https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/ , https://www.codeofhonor.com/blog/reducing-perceived-latency/ , https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-188 , https://wiki.factorio.com/Desync , https://veterannewb.itch.io/neon-marble-rust/devlog/764800/rollback-in-rts

- **Why lockstep:** sending state would cap AoE at ~250 moving units; sending only commands synchronises 1500 (8 players, 28.8k modem). All machines run an identical simulation; only inputs are exchanged. The MC port already has a deterministic fixed-tick GameState with a per-tick reference harness, which is exactly the prerequisite.
- **AoE's turn structure:** 200 ms "communication turns" decoupled from render frames; a command issued in turn N executes in turn N+2. Players did not notice 250 ms command latency, 250-500 ms "very playable", above 500 ms noticeable; *consistent* latency beats low-but-variable. Turn length adapted from measured ping and the slowest machine's frame time, increased quickly on spikes and decreased slowly. Players issued a command every 1.5-2 s normally, 3-4/s in fights; duplicate clicks on the same spot were filtered.
- **Determinism rules (AoE, Gaffer, Factorio):** no simulation dependence on local factors (frame time, settings, hardware); one synchronised RNG with identical call counts (AoE had to special-case terrain sounds); floating point is the usual culprit ("no silver bullet") so MC's all-integer fixed-point original simulation is a gift; keep the renderer and audio outside the simulation. Factorio: "You could play on a 500 ms connection and never desync. Latency causes rubber-banding and input delay, not desyncs."
- **Desync detection:** AoE checksummed world state every turn and found that tiny early divergences surface minutes later as resource discrepancies, so checksum *components* (unit positions, RNG state, resources) separately and log them; Factorio writes a desync report containing both states for diffing. For us: hash GameState per tick (or every 10 ticks), exchange hashes, and on mismatch dump the two GameStates to disk, which the existing reference_test diff tool can then compare. Keep a replay (seed + command stream) of every match for free bug reports.
- **Transport:** Gaffer's recipe: UDP, every packet carries *all* unacknowledged inputs (inputs compress to a few bytes), receiver acks, playout-delay buffer (~100 ms, adaptive) to absorb jitter; survives 2 s latency and 25% loss while TCP "behaves terribly".
- **Perceived latency (StarCraft/WC):** instant "Yes, milord" voice, selection ring and target marker on the local machine with no simulation change; the actual order lands 2 turns later.
- **Rollback vs lockstep:** rollback RTS (Neon Marble Rust) keeps a Display state and a Rollback state ~0.5 s behind, re-simulating 10 ticks in one frame on mismatch, which forced <1 ms per tick and limited the game to 1v1. Not appropriate for 1000+ Things and 4-8 wizards; use plain lockstep with 2-turn command delay plus latency masking. Only the wizard's own flight could use a thin client-side prediction layer if the 200-400 ms carpet steering feel is unacceptable, and the simplest way to do that is to make carpet movement intent-based (the sim moves toward a pointed direction) so the delay is barely felt.
- **Join-in-progress / rejoin:** Factorio handles desync by re-downloading the map; we already serialise GameState, so rejoin = send snapshot + replay commands since.

### 6.3 RTS bot architectures for a hobby project
Sources: https://keeperfx.net/wiki/Level-Script-Commands , https://www.hiveworkshop.com/threads/how-does-ai-difficulty-influence-ai-micro-and-unit-controls.294489 , https://github.com/SMUnlimited/AMAI/wiki , https://github.com/0ad/0ad/tree/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/ai/petra , https://www.gamedev.net/tutorials/programming/artificial-intelligence/the-core-mechanics-of-influence-mapping-r2799 , https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter30_Modular_Tactical_Influence_Maps.pdf , https://www.cs.mun.ca/~dchurchill/publications/pdf/aiide11-bo.pdf

- **Layered design that three shipped/mature bots agree on:**
  1. *Strategic layer* with a build/priority list: DK's processes+checks+events; WC3's `SetBuildUnit` build order and attack waves; Petra's `headquarters.js` deciding, `queueManager.js` allocating resources to prioritised `queue`s of `queueplanBuilding/Training/Research`, with `baseManager`, `attackManager`/`attackPlan`, `defenseManager`/`defenseArmy`, `emergencyManager`, `victoryManager`, `startingStrategy`, `config.js` (personality/difficulty).
  2. *Tactical layer*: influence maps. Each cell accumulates influence from friendly and enemy units with linear or inverse-distance falloff over a radius; derived maps: `influence = friendly - enemy`, `tension = friendly + enemy`, `vulnerability = tension - |influence|` (Champandard / Dave Mark). Use for "where is the enemy weak", "where should my elite hunt", "is my shrine threatened". Grid at MC's tile resolution, updated every ~0.5 s.
  3. *Unit layer*: finite state machine per squad (idle at lair -> move -> engage -> retreat when HP < 40% -> heal at castle), with a utility score to pick targets (distance, threat, value, is-wizard).
- **Difficulty:** scale by cheating (gather multipliers, as WC3 Insane and 0 A.D.) and by reaction time/decision interval, not by writing separate AIs. Hide the cheat in fiction.
- **Personality beats cleverness:** AMAI's personality profiles, chat and surrender; DK's 16 keeper models (aggressive/defensive/room-size quirks). Give each wizard bot a name, a favourite monster and spell, a preferred aggression and an expansion appetite; players remember "the one who always sends dragons at night".
- **Wizard-bot specifics:** the bot's avatar must *fly around and be seen*: patrol its shrines, creep elites for XP on a schedule, fly home when HP low, join its army's attacks. Scripted behaviour trees per phase (opening: claim 2 springs, build hive; mid: creep elites, raid weakest neighbour's villagers; late: desecration push) with the influence map choosing *where*.
- **Harassment and raids** are what WC3's stock AI lacked and AMAI added: a cheap, high-visibility raid routine (6 bees on the enemy's lumber yard every 3 minutes) makes a bot feel alive.
- **Build-order search** (Churchill & Buro, AIIDE 2011) is overkill for a 4-resource town; a hand-authored table per personality plus "if resource X is short, build its producer" suffices.

### 6.4 Procedural terrain for fair RTS maps
Sources: https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/ , https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/697004-providing-equal-opportunity-for-each-player/5380182/ , https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=396479 , https://www.moddb.com/news/generating-the-maps-of-trollskog , https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-would-i-go-around-identifying-islands-of-a-noise-generated-map/62997 , https://github.com/FAForever/Neroxis-Map-Generator

- **Heightfield:** 4 octaves of value/Perlin noise with amplitudes 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 (different seed or offset per octave to avoid correlation), then `e = pow(e * 1.2, 2..4)` to flatten lowlands and sharpen peaks, then terrace to MC's height quantisation. MC terrain is a heightfield already (and the original generator is in the exe), so this slots in; the water line and lava can be two thresholds. Separate moisture noise picks biome palette (grass/sand/snow tmaps).
- **Fairness:** generate a half or quarter map and mirror/rotate it (2-player: 8 possible mirror/rotate layouts; 4-player: mirror a quarter both ways; N-player: point-symmetric wedges), so every start has identical terrain and resource distances (gamedev.net and Factorio threads). Neroxis (Supreme Commander) is the mature reference: pick a symmetry (point/mirror/spawn count), place spawns first, then resource points in distance bands from spawns, then shape terrain to that layout. MC's world wraps on a torus, which makes point symmetry about the torus centre especially clean.
- **Resource placement by distance band:** each start gets the same count of mana springs within R1 (safe), contested springs on the symmetry axes at R2, and the elite-guarded "rich" springs in the centre. Wild lairs graded 1-5 by distance from the nearest spawn (WC3 creep-camp rule). Towns need flat buildable plots: ensure a flat disc (say 24x24 tiles) at each spawn by blending the noise toward a plateau height with a radial mask (Red Blob island-shaping trick inverted).
- **Connectivity:** flood-fill / connected-component labelling the walkable mask (land above water, below lava, slope under the walk threshold) and require all spawns and all springs to be in one component, otherwise re-roll or carve a ramp along the shortest Dijkstra path. Also Dijkstra from each spawn to every objective to verify *equal path lengths within 10%* for symmetric maps (a sanity check rather than a guarantee). Ground monsters need this; flyers do not, which is a design lever (worms cannot cross water, dragons can).
- **Distribution of decoration and lairs:** Poisson-disc sampling (Trollskog, Red Blob) rather than high-frequency noise so nothing clumps; Voronoi regions seeded by Poisson points give natural "provinces" for Northgard-style territory tiles and for placing wild encampments at region crossroads.
- **Scale:** 12-16x a MC level area means 4x the linear size; keep the generator chunked and seed-deterministic (same seed = same map on every client, which lockstep requires, and the seed is part of the replay).

## 7. Design lessons, ranked by expected impact on retention and replayability

1. **Make death cheap and the castle the real objective** (Sacrifice altar, WC3 revive, Populous reincarnation). Wizard death = respawn at castle in 10 s + 5 s/level; elimination only via a channelled, interruptible *desecration* of the castle. Removes the "lost one duel, lost the game" feel that killed Sacrifice multiplayer and that MC's own single-wizard duels have.
2. **Essence/soul economy instead of a population cap** (Sacrifice). Summons cost essence that returns on death unless an enemy converts the corpse within a window. Creates a mid-fight objective, a natural army cap, and a comeback path. Pair with a WC3-style upkeep drain above two thresholds to keep armies small and the wizard relevant.
3. **Rarity-tiered wild monsters with visible affixes and guaranteed drops** (D2/PoE). Named elites with 2-3 affixes from MC's own effects list, colour-coded health bars, drop beams, floating damage numbers, "at least magic" guaranteed drops, a hidden pity counter. This is the "come back tomorrow" hook and it costs little simulation work.
4. **Territory via claimable mana springs + influence radius** (Sacrifice manaliths, B&W influence, Northgard tiles). Springs are what bots fight over, what alternate win conditions count, and what a BR-style blight can revoke. Influence radius bounds building and makes spells cheaper at home.
5. **Thin, autonomous RTS layer** (Majesty, DK imps, AoE idle button, Tooth and Tail). Villagers self-assign; buildings go on fixed plots; monsters guard and retreat on their own; the player gives four orders from first person and uses the overview for groups and rally flags. Every reviewer complaint about Sacrifice and SpellForce was about attention load; design for a player who is flying a carpet.
6. **Building as recruitment filter, levels earned in combat** (DK). Town buildings unlock monster types at the lair rather than a tech tree; monsters above level 4 can only be made by surviving fights, producing veteran units players name and protect.
7. **Scheduled global pressure** (Northgard winter, BR ring, WC3 night). A day/night cycle where wild monsters sleep by day and raids are blind at night, plus an optional escalating blight for BR matches, gives every match phases and a hard stop around 30-40 minutes.
8. **Pre-match loadout + in-match discovery** (Sacrifice spellbook, Populous stone heads, D3 smart loot). Choose 6 spells to bring; the rest only drop from elites, biased toward what you carry. Both build variety and a reason to hunt.
9. **Bots with personality, cheats hidden in fiction** (AMAI, DK keeper models, WC3 Insane). Named wizard bots with preferred monsters, aggression and raid habits; difficulty via gather multipliers and reaction intervals.
10. **Replays and desync dumps from day one** (AoE, Factorio). Deterministic sim + command log = free replays, free bug reports, and shareable "look at this match" moments that feed an online community.

## 8. Netcode and AI summary for the roadmap

- **Netcode:** deterministic lockstep, 4-8 players, fixed sim tick (MC's existing tick) grouped into 200 ms command turns with a 2-turn execution delay; adaptive turn length; UDP with redundant input packets and ~100 ms playout buffer; per-tick (or per-10-tick) component checksums exchanged, mismatch -> dump both GameStates for reference_test diffing; instant local acknowledgement (sound + marker) to hide the delay; snapshot + command replay for rejoin. Avoid rollback: the unit count and player count make it impractical, and MC's integer simulation makes lockstep easy. Keep all randomness in the shared RNG and all rendering/audio outside the sim (the port already enforces this split).
- **AI:** three layers. (1) Strategic: DK-style prioritised *processes* fed by periodic *checks* and pre-empted by *events*, parameterised per personality (build table, aggression, expansion appetite, raid cadence, favourite monsters). (2) Tactical: tile-grid influence maps (friendly, enemy, tension, vulnerability) updated twice a second drive where armies, raids and the wizard avatar go. (3) Unit: FSM per squad with retreat thresholds and a utility target picker. Difficulty = resource multiplier + decision interval. Avatar behaviour trees per match phase so the bot wizard is visibly present (creeping elites, claiming springs, defending the castle, flying home when hurt).
- **Procgen:** symmetric (mirror/point) seeded generation of octave noise with exponent redistribution and terraces; spawns first, resources in distance bands, flat plateau at each spawn, Poisson-disc wild lairs graded by distance, flood-fill connectivity with carve-or-reroll, equal-path sanity check; seed travels in the lobby and in the replay.
